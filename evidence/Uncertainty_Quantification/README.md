# Uncertainty_Quantification — uncertainty quantification (synthetic data)
Run `pip install -r requirements.txt`, then `python Uncertainty_Quantification.py /path/to/Supply_Chain_CSV`. The frozen Predictive_Modelling artefact is included. To predict new orders: `python score_new.py input.csv output.csv`. Compatible order-time predictors and unique PO/line keys are required; maintain ID strings including leading zeroes. Only load trusted joblib files. A replay of all 1,000 scoring records matched the generated predictions; new categorical levels are flagged in the scoring script.

## Design
The 6,000 training observations fit three gradient boosting quantile models (0.1, 0.5, 0.9). Existing Predictive_Modelling Ridge/logistic models stay frozen. The 1,000 validation cases calibrate pooled absolute residual intervals, separate sea/non-sea absolute residual intervals, conservative nonnegative conformalized quantile regression (CQR) expansions, and a sigmoid calibration of logistic scores. Quantile crossing is corrected by row sorting. Finite-sample empirical residual order statistic is ceil((n+1)*nominal coverage). Lower endpoints are clipped at zero. The 2,000 test cases evaluate, never fit. The 1,000 unlabelled scoring cases demonstrate deployment. The validation set was previously used to choose Predictive_Modelling models, and the test set was already inspected in earlier analyses; neither is a genuinely fresh independent evaluation. Temporal shift and dependence also limit formal conformal coverage guarantees.

## Findings and operational recommendation
On the test set group-specific 80% and 90% intervals achieved 79.15% and 88.45% coverage with mean widths 11.42 and 14.57 days. Sea coverage rose from 55.70% to 79.14% at nominal 80%. Group intervals are recommended provisionally for decision support because they are interpretable and their interval scores are favourable; confirm with new data before operational deployment. Group radii are 8.79/11.39 days for sea and 4.78/6.06 for non-sea. CQR is retained as an alternative. Platt scaling marginally improved log loss but did not improve held-out Brier score; keep raw logistic probabilities as default pending independent calibration, and expose calibrated probabilities for comparison. Risk bands in exported data are illustrative and use calibrated probabilities (<40%, 40–<75%, >=75%); no cost-based threshold was validated. Quantile bounds and classifier probabilities arise from different models and need not be mutually coherent. Do not assume a full probability distribution from three quantiles or two intervals. For Prescriptive_Analytics use explicitly documented scenarios or fit a suitable conditional distribution with independent evaluation. No real German enterprise data, deep learning, R, Shiny, or SAP deployment was performed. Data remain synthetic. No outcome is known for scoring orders.
